How to Organize Receipts for Tax Season

A shoebox of faded receipts is the last thing anyone wants to deal with in April. Here's a simpler way to get them organized — and exportable — well before the deadline.

Why Receipt Organization Matters for Taxes

Deductible expenses only help if you can actually substantiate them — a credit card statement showing a charge at an office supply store doesn't tell your accountant (or the CRA/IRS, if you're ever asked) what was actually purchased. The itemized receipt does. The problem is that thermal-printed receipts fade within months, which means the paper trail for a deduction you claim in April can be unreadable by the time anyone asks about it.

Digitizing receipts as soon as you get them — rather than tossing them in a drawer to deal with later — solves both problems: the data survives even after the paper fades, and it's already in a spreadsheet by the time tax season starts.

A Simple Year-Round Routine

1. Scan receipts the same day

Photograph or upload each receipt right after the purchase, while the paper is still readable. It takes seconds and means nothing gets lost by December.

2. Use a dedicated project

Create a project (e.g. '2026 Taxes') just for deductible purchases, separate from personal spending, so the year-end export is already filtered to what your accountant needs.

3. Check the extracted totals

Spot-check that the subtotal, tax, and total match the photo, especially on longer receipts — a quick glance takes less time than re-entering everything by hand later.

4. Export before you file

Download a CSV for your own records or a formatted PDF report to hand directly to your accountant — both are generated instantly, whenever you need them.

Getting a Clean Scan From a Faded Receipt

  • Flatten the receipt fully — curled thermal paper casts shadows that can obscure line items
  • Use even, indirect lighting rather than direct flash, which washes out faint thermal print
  • Photograph faded receipts as soon as possible — thermal ink degrades further every month
  • For long receipts, make sure the total and tax lines at the bottom are fully in frame
